# THISTLEWOOD'S — Episode 1 · "The Wrong Tune"
### Pilot script v3.1 — THE FAMILY CUT · target runtime 11:00 · SUPERSEDES v1, v2 & v3
*(v3.1: Castle Arcade address — the in-world arcade name is now Castle Arcade, aligned to Story Bible v4.)*

*A Cre8tive Production · 2D animation · Ages 7–11 + parent co-viewing.*
*Canon: Richard (grandfather); his four grandchildren — LILY (the eldest, the Captain), ROSE (the Heart's Eye), PEGGI (the littlest, the Spark), ALBERT (the Chronicler). Equal four-hander. The pilot is an ensemble episode: all four go through. Timing markers `[m:ss]` keep it honest to 11:00.*

---

## COLD OPEN `[0:00 – 1:20]`

**EXT. CARDIFF — CASTLE STREET — MORNING** `[0:00]`

Fine Welsh rain. The castle, ancient among the buses. Between two shopfronts: a narrow iron arch, easy to miss. The camera drifts through — and grey rain turns to gold.

**INT. CASTLE ARCADE — CONTINUOUS**

Honey light under a glass roof. Macsen the cat asleep in the bookshop window — the first shop on the right. Past him, past the little music shop, three shops up on the right: gold letters that have never faded: **A. THISTLEWOOD — RESTORATIONS & CURIOSITIES. EST. 1922.**

**INT. THISTLEWOOD'S — WORKSHOP — CONTINUOUS** `[0:35]`

RICHARD, on a stepladder at the BACK DOOR — old oak, iron straps curling into a shape like a letter *T* — makes a tiny adjustment to a brass panel in the frame. Pleased with himself.

Across the room: ALBERT faces the door, stopwatch up, notebook open, deadly serious. PEGGI sits cross-legged on the workbench eating an apple, watching him like a slow bus. LILY leans on the shelves, arms folded, one scuffed knee. ROSE, in the good chair, is drawing Macsen from memory.

**ALBERT:** Attempt forty-seven. Conditions: normal. Barometric pressure —

**PEGGI** *(mouth full)*: Probably fine.

**ALBERT** *(wounded)*: The System is *delicate*.

He knocks a careful rhythm on the door. Nothing. Consults notebook. Different rhythm. Nothing.

**PEGGI:** There's nothing on the bench.

Albert looks at the bench — one girl, one apple, no antiques. Closes the stopwatch with dignity.

**ALBERT:** …The System also knew that.

**RICHARD** *(not turning)*: The door doesn't open for cleverness. Never has. *(climbing down, pleased)* And I've very nearly got the *clothes* sorted. One more tweak and it'll dress you properly.

**LILY:** You said that last week, Grandad.

**RICHARD:** And I'm *closer* than I was last week.

He is not. The door knows it. The shop BELL rings.

**TITLE: THISTLEWOOD'S — "The Wrong Tune"** `[1:20]`

## ACT ONE `[1:20 – 4:00]`

**INT. THISTLEWOOD'S — FRONT ROOM** `[1:25]`

TOM CHEN (30s, gentle, lost) holds something wrapped in a tea towel like a sleeping hedgehog. Macsen has relocated to the counter to judge him.

**TOM:** Are you Mr Thistlewood? A lady at the market said — if it's broken, it belongs here.

**RICHARD:** Somebody's been reading my sign.

Tom unwraps a **music box** — worn walnut, small brass key, a little worn place on the lid where a thumb has rested for a hundred years. He sets it down like an apology.

**TOM:** It was my gran's. Hers before that, right back. She left it to me with one instruction. *Have it mended. I want it to play again.* Only — it's never played *right*. Listen.

He winds it. A French tune spills out — sweet, and *wrong*. The same notes bent the same way, every time. The four children materialise at the counter, drawn by it.

**PEGGI** *(delighted)*: Ooh, that's *terrible*.

**ALBERT** *(already writing)*: It's not terrible, it's — *specific*. The same notes are wrong every single time.

**ROSE** *(not looking at the mechanism — looking at the worn place on the lid, softly)*: Somebody loved this. Loved it *loads*.

**TOM:** Three menders looked at it. Two said scrap it. One said the mechanism's *fighting itself* — built wrong on purpose. *(quiet)* I just want to do what she asked. And I can't even get it to play a proper song.

Richard ties his **apron** — over the head, tied at back, both hands smoothing the front — and lifts the box in his broad careful hands. He goes very still. His eyes change, just a little. He knows something. He doesn't say what.

**RICHARD** *(to the box more than to Tom)*: Nothing is truly broken. It just needs some TLC. *(to Tom)* Come back at collecting time.

**TOM:** Don't you need to know anything about it?

**RICHARD:** I expect I'll find out.

The bell rings Tom out. And the BACK DOOR exhales — a thin seam of *other* light around its edge.

**ALBERT** *(hushed)*: It's open.

Richard slides the drawer, lifts out the **compass** — brass, needle asleep — and hands it to Albert like a coat. Then reaches up and gives the brass panel one last hopeful tweak.

**RICHARD:** *This* time the clothes will be spot on. I've recalibrated.

**LILY** *(already rolling up her sleeves)*: What's missing this time, Grandad?

**RICHARD** *(back at his loupe)*: A small thing. You'll know it when you understand it. One hour.

Lily grabs the brass ring two-handed and heaves. Golden light pours in.

**ROSE** *(breathless)*: Together?

**ALL FOUR:** Together.

They step through. Richard watches the door close. Under his breath, almost nothing:

**RICHARD:** Don't be long. `[4:00]`

## ACT TWO `[4:00 – 9:00]`

**EXT. FRENCH VILLAGE — MARKET SQUARE — DAY — 1867** `[4:00]`

Sun, dust, bread, geese. A Burgundy market in full cry. And four children in catastrophically wrong outfits.

ALBERT: a Parisian dandy twenty years out of date — velvet coat, a cane he can't hold, a tall hat with THREE ribbons. The hat falls off. He catches it. It falls off again. A goose takes an interest.

PEGGI: a Dutch farm girl — bonnet, striped apron, enormous wooden clogs.

**PEGGI:** Why am I *Dutch*?

ROSE: a fine Parisian lady in miniature — ivory silk, rosebuds, gloves, parasol. She turns a slow circle, watching the skirt swirl.

**ROSE** *(anguished)*: Oh no. It's beautiful. It's completely wrong and it's the most beautiful thing I've ever worn. I hate how much I love it.

LILY: the tomboy of the family — in a city gown of bows, flounces, ringlets, and little heeled shoes. A thundercloud in lace.

**LILY:** I have never worn anything with this many bows. I want it noted that I object.

**THE OTHER THREE:** Noted.

Peggi kicks the clogs off in four seconds and tucks them under her arm. Albert produces the compass — needle dead, as always, until they *understand*.

**ALBERT:** Right. We grid the square. Systematic sweep, north to —

But Lily is up on her toes — heels and all — scanning over the crowd like a captain scanning the sea.

**LILY:** Or we follow the music.

They all hear it then. From a lane off the square: a tune. Sweet, French — and wrong.

**EXT. CRAFTSMAN'S WORKSHOP — CONTINUOUS** `[5:00]`

A beautiful little workshop being emptied. Crates on a handcart. A BUYER ticking a list. In the doorway, planted like a small tree in a gale: PASCAL, 14, red-eyed, clutching a wrapped bundle.

**PASCAL:** Please — just till this evening — I haven't finished his last —

**BUYER:** Sold is sold, lad. Cart leaves when it's loaded.

Peggi walks straight in. Albert winces — *you can't just —*

**PEGGI:** Hello. Are you the one who mends the music?

**PASCAL** *(hollow)*: I'm the one whose master made toys that *don't work*. And now he's gone. And they're taking the lot.

He unwraps the bundle: a half-finished music box. Winds it. The SAME wrong tune. The four go still.

**PEGGI:** That's our song.

**PASCAL** *(cracking)*: It's *nobody's* song. Every box my master built plays it wrong — dozens, all his life. The buyers laughed at him. And this is the last one he ever started, and I can't finish it, because there's a piece missing and I don't know which — and what's the *point*, if it only plays it *wrong*?

Albert flips madly through his notebook, comparing wrong notes to diagrams. Compass: dead. But ROSE isn't looking at the box. She's looking at Pascal — at his thumb resting on the lid, on exactly the spot where, on a much older box in Cardiff, a hundred years of thumbs have worn the varnish smooth.

**ROSE** *(quietly, to the others)*: He's not angry. Look at his face. He's *heartbroken*. You don't get heartbroken over a toy that doesn't work.

And PEGGI — who has never once been too polite to ask — sits down on a crate beside Pascal and asks the plain, true question everyone clever skipped.

**PEGGI:** Whose song is it?

Beat. Nobody has ever asked him that.

**PASCAL:** …There's a story. Years ago — an English soldier, passing through. At this market he heard a girl singing. He didn't speak French. So he learned her song by ear — and he got it wrong. Every note, a little wrong, the way you do when you love a thing and can't quite reach it. And when his war was over he came back, and asked my master to build a box that played it. My master said, *I know that song, monsieur. I can build it properly.*

**ROSE** *(whisper)*: And the soldier said no.

**PASCAL:** The soldier said no. *Not the real song. Her song. The way I heard it.* Her song — in his mistakes. My master built the wrongness in on purpose. The most careful wrongness he ever made. And after she was gone, he kept building it. Box after box. All his life. Everyone thought it was madness.

**PEGGI** *(the mighty frown of a small girl doing large thinking)*: So it's not broken. It was never *meant* to be right. It's exactly as wrong as he loved her.

**THE COMPASS WAKES.** The needle SLAMS round and points at the corner crate. The outer ring begins, faintly, to glow. Albert stares at it, then at Peggi. His System quietly falls over.

**ALBERT:** I was measuring the notes. You just — *asked him*.

**PEGGI** *(kindly)*: You can't grid a feeling, Albert.

**THE COMPLICATION** `[6:45]` — The CARTER hoists that exact crate onto the cart. The Buyer slaps the side. The cart rolls.

**PEGGI:** The piece is in *that crate!*

**ALBERT** *(hat falling off)*: The cart's *leaving!*

**LILY** *(the eldest-of-four emergency voice, kicking off her heels, hitching an armful of lace)*: RIGHT. Albert — left side. Rose, Peggi — with me. GO!

THE CHASE — through the market, and the market never forgets it: Lily vaulting a fruit stall, bows and all (an old woman selling apricots applauds); Albert attempting the next stall and *joining* it (the goose arrives at speed to press its advantage); Rose running in ivory silk, apologising beautifully — "*Pardon! So sorry! Lovely plums!*" — and PEGGI, smallest and fastest, hurling a wooden clog that skips once off the cobbles and jams — CLUNK — into the back wheel's spokes. The cart judders to a stop at the bridge. The carter looks at the clog, looks at the four of them, sits down on a milestone and lights his pipe, defeated by the day.

Lily heaves the crate open. Hearts sink: DOZENS of brass cams, identical, glinting in the shavings. The compass ring glows brighter. Time.

**PEGGI** *(digging)*: Which one?! They all look the same!

And Albert grows half an inch on the inside — no grid, no System. He uses what Peggi taught him ten minutes ago.

**ALBERT:** They don't all look the same. One of them looks *wrong*. His master cut the mistakes into the teeth — carefully — on purpose. We're not looking for a perfect one. We're looking for the crooked one.

Everyone digs. And it's ROSE — of course it's Rose, who has spent her whole life seeing what everyone else walks past — who lifts one small cam to the light. Its teeth are deliberately, delicately, *beautifully* uneven.

**ROSE:** This one. It's the only one that's — *meant*.

**PASCAL** *(catching up, taking it with shaking hands)*: That's his cutting. That's *it*.

The ring flares. The hour is nearly done. Lily pushes the cam into Pascal's hands and grins her captain's grin.

**LILY:** Finish the box. Finish *all* of them. Your master wasn't the old man who couldn't tune a comb. He was the only one in the whole world playing it right.

They bolt for a lane where the light has turned golden and strange.

**PASCAL** *(shouting after)*: Wait — WHO ARE YOU?

**PEGGI** *(hopping into her one remaining clog)*: DUTCH, apparently! `[9:00]`

## ACT THREE `[9:00 – 10:45]`

**INT. THISTLEWOOD'S — WORKSHOP — CONTINUOUS** `[9:00]`

The door bangs open; four children tumble out in ordinary clothes and extraordinary spirits. Peggi is, inexplicably, still holding one dented clog. *(She checks. It fits perfectly. She decides not to mention it. Yet.)*

Richard doesn't turn. The music box waits open under the lamp — and in his fingers, already, is the crooked little cam. As if he'd known all along exactly which piece it was, and had simply been waiting for them to bring home the part of the story you can't hold in your hand.

**RICHARD:** Tea's gone cold.

**ALBERT** *(gasping)*: Grandad — the notes are *meant* to be wrong — a soldier — a girl singing — he loved her, so the maker built the wrongness in on *purpose* —

**RICHARD** *(fitting the cam; a click no louder than a ladybird landing)*: I know.

**ALL FOUR:** You *knew*?

He winds the key. The wrong tune fills the workshop — whole now, every crooked note exactly where a soldier's heart put it — and in the dusty gold light it is the most beautiful thing any of them has ever heard. He listens to all of it. Only then does he look at them over his glasses.

**RICHARD:** I didn't know the *story*. The apron only ever tells me what matters. *(the rare smile, quick as a bird)* You four brought back the *why*.

**ROSE** *(slowly, in the good chair)*: The girl in the story. The one who sang. Tom said his gran's family were French. From near Burgundy.

**ALBERT** *(already worked out, already written down)*: It's her box. It was always her box. His great-great-grandmother.

**LILY:** And he'll never know.

**RICHARD** *(polishing the worn place on the lid, round and round)*: No. *(beat)* That's the job.

**INT. THISTLEWOOD'S — FRONT ROOM — LATER** `[10:05]`

Tom Chen at the counter, tea towel folded under his arm just in case.

**TOM:** You fixed it? It plays properly now?

**RICHARD:** It plays the way it was always meant to.

Tom winds the key. The wrong tune — the right tune — fills the little shop. His face does something complicated. He doesn't understand why a crooked melody makes his chest go tight. Something under the wrong notes is simply *there* — like a voice from another room. Like home.

**TOM** *(quiet, touching the worn place on the lid the way his gran did, the way her gran did, the way a girl at a market once did)*: My gran's family were French, you know. Way back. Burgundy somewhere, she used to say. …I just wish I knew why she loved it so much.

He doesn't look up. So he never sees it:

Richard. Lily. Rose. Peggi. Albert.

**Five pairs of eyes. Five secret smiles.**

The bell rings him out into the honey light, carrying a love song he'll never fully know the words to. Macsen watches him all the way to the arch.

**PEGGI:** We can never tell them, can we. Not ever.

**RICHARD** *(picking up the next broken thing; reaching for his apron)*: No, cariad. That's the job.

Across the arcade, precisely as the castle clock says three, Mrs Patel crosses with a tray of tea — for a shop that keeps its own time. `[10:45]`

**SMASH TO BLACK.**

---

## TAG (post-credits) `[10:45 – 11:00]`

Albert's notebook, close, by lamplight. Under **"PEGGI'S RULE: you can't grid a feeling"**, a new heading in his carefulest letters: **"THINGS THAT DON'T BEHAVE NORMALLY."**

*1. The gold paint tin. Never gets emptier. Checked twice. Drew lines. Same.*
*2. Peggi's clogs fit. Everything the door gives us is wrong. Except our shoes. Why do our shoes fit?*

His pencil hovers. On the high shelf, half-hidden behind the solvent jars: a small wooden box that has been there longer than Albert has been alive. Tonight, Grandad looked at it — half a second — before turning out the light.

*3. The little box on Grandad's shelf.*

Off-page: a CRASH, and Peggi's voice — "*Macsen, we've talked about this.*" Albert closes the notebook.

**RICHARD** *(O.S.)*: Albert. Your grandmother's clock says quarter past. It's half three. *(beat)* …I'll have a look at *that* next.

**END OF EPISODE.** `[11:00]`

### The four gifts, load-bearing (why this is the family cut)

**Lily** captains the chase and gives Pascal the parting gift. **Rose** reads the grief and finds the crooked cam. **Peggi** asks the question that wakes the compass. **Albert** maps the wrongness and grows past his System. Nobody is the star. The family is.
