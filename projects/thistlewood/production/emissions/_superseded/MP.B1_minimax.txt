Hand-drawn 2D animation: warm coloured-ink linework in browns, ambers and muted greens, no black outlines, flat colour on grained paper texture, honeyed lamplight, cosy storybook mood with quiet melancholy. No 3D, no photorealism.

An antique restorer's workshop. The camera pushes in slowly and steadily toward the workbench while four small tasks continue at once: the smallest girl in blue overalls climbs the last of the way onto a wooden stool and settles; the girl with the red braid, far back at the shelves, presses a jar into place and lowers her arms; the boy in the brown jumper walks slowly across the room writing in his open notebook, eyes down; the white-bearded grandfather in the leather apron carries a small walnut music box in both hands to the workbench and sets it down. Nobody hurries; only the girl in the green cardigan watches him.

The camera moves in close on his hands and the box. He lifts the lid onto a brass cylinder and a steel comb with a clear crack across it, turns the small brass key twice, then lifts both hands away. The cylinder turns and the comb sounds three halting notes, the first two clear and the third sour and wrong.

The camera pulls back to hold the whole room. Stillness arrives one child at a time and never all at once: the girl in the green cardigan is already looking; the boy's pencil stops and his head comes up; the girl with the red braid turns from the shelves; the smallest girl stops last, mouth still open mid-word. Four faces come to rest on the box at four different speeds. The grandfather does not look up from it and speaks quietly to the object itself. The room then holds completely still: five figures motionless around the lit workbench, the open music box at centre frame, the old oak door dark at the rear.

The five keep the identities, hair, clothing and relative heights of the opening image throughout; no one else enters; there is only one music box.
