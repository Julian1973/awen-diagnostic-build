An old man with white hair, a beard and round glasses stands behind the counter, listening, in shirtsleeves and a green waistcoat. A ginger cat sits upright on the counter.

The camera pushes in slowly and steadily toward the counter across the whole shot, and never cuts.

First: the man in the parka sets the wrapped bundle down on the counter and folds the cloth open, revealing a small worn walnut music box with a brass latch. Then: the brass cylinder begins to turn. The old man does not move; he is listening.

Then: four children come to the counter, drawn by the sound, arriving from the left at different moments and at different distances — a very small girl with two dark braids in blue overalls up on her toes with both hands on the counter edge; a boy in a brown jumper beside her writing in an open notebook; a girl in a green cardigan standing a little further back who looks at the LID of the box rather than at the mechanism; and a taller girl with a long red braid and a green jacket furthest back with her arms folded, watching the man in the parka's face instead of the box.
