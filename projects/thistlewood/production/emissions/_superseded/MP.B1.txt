@Image 1 is the first frame.
This first frame defines the workshop, the position and pose of every character, their designs and clothing, the workbench and its lamp, and the camera direction. The video begins naturally from this frame.

[Characters]
The five characters are the group in @Image 1: the grandfather in the leather work apron, the boy in the brown jumper, the girl with the red braid, the girl in the green cardigan, and the smallest girl in blue overalls. Use only their faces, hair and clothing as shown. The grandfather stands a head and shoulders taller than any of the children. Among the children the smallest girl in blue overalls is shortest, then the girl in the green cardigan, then the girl with the red braid, and the boy in the brown jumper is tallest by a small margin.

[Props]
The music box corresponds to @Image 2: a small walnut box with a brass latch, its lid opening onto a brass cylinder and a steel comb with a clear crack across it. Use only the object from @Image 2; do not use its background. One music box throughout: the grandfather carries it to the workbench, sets it down, and it stays there.

[Scenes]
The workshop references @Image 1: shelves, an old radio, a stove and kettle on the left wall; a heavy workbench and a brick window on the right; an old oak door with a black iron strap centred in the stone rear wall; one lamp over the bench.

[Visual Style]
Hand-drawn 2D animation. Warm coloured-ink linework in browns, ambers and muted greens — never black outlines. Flat colour fills with a subtle paper texture, as if painted on lightly grained stock. Soft amber-warm palette with honeyed light. Characters are simple, expressive shapes with large readable eyes and distinctive silhouettes; their movement is gentle and slightly springy, drawn on twos. Backgrounds are richly detailed painted interiors rendered flat with painted depth, no perspective tricks. Cosy storybook atmosphere with quiet melancholy underneath. No 3D rendering, no photorealism, no painterly brushwork, no cel-shine gradients. The workshop is a working restorer's room of worn timber, brass and tools.

[Audio and Dialogue]
Audio is diegetic only — the sounds actually present in the room. Dialogue language: English. Only the grandfather speaks, in a warm unhurried Welsh rhythm, going quieter as the line matters more; the children listen with their mouths naturally closed.

[Event Script]
[Stage 1]
Opening state: the room exactly as in the first frame. Primary event: the camera pushes in slowly and steadily toward the workbench while four small tasks continue at once — the smallest girl in blue overalls climbs the last of the way onto a wooden stool and settles on it; the girl with the red braid, far back at the shelves, presses a jar into place and lowers her arms; the boy in the brown jumper walks slowly across the room writing in his open notebook, eyes down; the grandfather carries the music box in both hands to the workbench and sets it down. Nobody hurries; only the girl in the green cardigan watches the grandfather. <a kettle ticking, a pencil moving, footsteps on old boards> End state: the music box rests on the bench under the lamp, the grandfather's hands still either side of it, each child still at their own task.

[Stage 2]
Continue from the previous stage: the same room, lamp and positions. Primary event: the camera moves in close on the grandfather's hands and the music box. He lifts the lid, turns the small brass key twice, then lifts both hands away. The lid stands open, the brass cylinder turns, and the steel comb sounds three halting notes — the first two clear and sweet, the third sour and wrong, dying away. <three halting music-box notes, the third off-key, then silence> End state: the music box is open and turning under the lamp, nobody touching it.

[Stage 3]
Continue from the previous stage: the music box open on the bench, the grandfather's hands away from it. Primary event: the camera pulls back to hold the whole room. Stillness arrives one child at a time and never all at once — the girl in the green cardigan is already looking; the boy's pencil stops on the page and his head comes up; the girl with the red braid turns from the shelves; the smallest girl stops last, her mouth still open mid-word. Four faces come to rest on the box at four different speeds. The grandfather does not look up from it, and says quietly, as if asking the object itself: {Now then… what happened to you?} After the line the room holds completely still. End state: five figures motionless around the lit workbench, the open music box at the centre of frame, the oak door dark and unremarked at the rear.

[Maintain Consistency]
Keep every character's identity, hair and clothing as in the first frame, and the same five people present throughout. Keep the relative heights above in every shot, one music box with its stated ownership, and the workshop layout, bench-lamp key and window fill unchanged from first frame to last.
