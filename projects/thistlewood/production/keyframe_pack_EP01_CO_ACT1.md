# EP01 · KEYFRAME PACK — cold open & Act One
Nine keyframes covering 0:00–4:00. Generated in the image tool, SEE-gated by
Julian, then each becomes the single reference image for its shot.

## Read this first
**Each keyframe is a FIRST FRAME.** It shows where the shot *begins*, never
where it ends — specify the stillness and the video plays backwards. Caught
once on TP-03; now law.

**One reference image per shot.** Identity cannot arrive from a separate model
sheet, so every character who appears must already be drawn into the keyframe,
correct and on-model. That is why there are nine of these and not two plates.

**Scale is directed by body landmarks, never centimetres** — models cannot see
numbers. The ladder, largest gap first:
- Peggi's head reaches **Rose's shoulder** (the biggest step)
- Rose's head reaches **Lily's ear**
- Lily is **barely a nose shorter** than Albert (the smallest step)
- Richard is **a head and shoulders** above every child

**Appearance locks, every frame:** Richard — white hair and beard, round
glasses, leather work apron. Albert — brown hair, brown jumper, notebook and
pencil, tallest child. Lily — long red braid, green field jacket. Rose —
strawberry-blonde, green cardigan, floral dress. Peggi — two dark braids,
striped shirt, blue overalls, smallest.

**Anti-lineup, every multi-character frame:** stage at three depths, give each
character one physical verb bound to a named object, and state that they are
not standing in a row and not all facing the same way.

**Style block** — paste verbatim at the end of every prompt below:
> Hand-drawn 2D animation. Warm coloured-ink linework in browns, ambers and muted greens — never black outlines. Flat colour fills with a subtle paper texture, as if painted on lightly grained stock. Soft amber-warm palette with honeyed light. Characters are simple, expressive shapes with large readable eyes and distinctive silhouettes; their movement is gentle and slightly springy, drawn on twos. Backgrounds are richly detailed painted interiors rendered flat with painted depth, no perspective tricks. Cosy storybook atmosphere with quiet melancholy underneath. No 3D rendering, no photorealism, no painterly brushwork, no cel-shine gradients. 16:9, full resolution.

**Sign strings, now canonical:** fascia board reads
`A. THISTLEWOOD — RESTORATIONS & CURIOSITIES · EST. 1922`; window glass and
title card read `Thistlewood's`.

## KF-CO.2 — Attempt Forty-Seven
Reference: the approved CLEAN WORKSHOP plate, door **unlit** (ordinary, dormant).
Match its architecture exactly — shelves with the radio and jars left, stove and
kettle left of centre, oak door with the capital-T strap and separate lower
strap centred in the stone rear wall, tool rack and workbench right, brick
window right, bench lamp. Do not redesign the room.

Five characters, staged at three depths, each mid-task and none of them looking
at each other:

**Foreground right, nearest camera** — PEGGI sits cross-legged **on top of the
workbench**, an apple half-eaten in one hand, chin up, watching Albert with the
patient expression of someone watching a very slow bus. Her head would reach
Rose's shoulder if they stood together; she is plainly the smallest.

**Midground centre** — ALBERT stands square to the old oak door, a stopwatch
held up in his left hand, an open notebook in his right, pencil behind his ear.
Deadly serious. He is the tallest of the four children, barely a nose above
Lily. His back is three-quarters to camera; we read the door past his shoulder.

**Midground left** — LILY leans against the shelves, arms folded, weight on one
hip, one knee scuffed. Bored and unimpressed. Her head reaches just above
Rose's ear.

**Background left, by the brick window** — ROSE sits in the good chair, a
drawing board on her knees, pencil down on the paper, looking at her drawing
and not at the room.

**Background right, raised** — RICHARD stands near the top of a wooden
stepladder set beside the doorframe, reaching up with a very small screwdriver
to a small brass panel set into the frame. His back is to the room. Leather
apron. He is a head and shoulders taller than any of the children.

They are not standing in a row, they are not all facing the same way, and no
two of them are looking at each other. **The workbench is otherwise EMPTY — no
antiques, no music box, nothing under repair.** That emptiness is the joke and
the plot: the door will not open because there is nothing on the bench.

Light: bench lamp warm on the right, cool grey daylight from the brick window,
honey lamplight overall. The oak door is dark and inert — **no glow, no seam of
light.** Its moment is later.

## KF-A1.1 — If it's broken, it belongs here
Reference: the approved PUBLIC SHOP (Front Room) interior master. Match it
exactly — the counter rear right facing the entrance, the arch through to the
workshop with warm light beyond, the glazed front door with its bell, the
curved bow window, floor-to-ceiling shelves of curiosities. Do not redesign the

**Foreground, outside the counter** — TOM CHEN, a gentle man in his thirties,
ordinary modern coat, standing slightly too far back from the counter as if
unsure he is in the right place. He holds a bundle wrapped in a tea towel in
both hands, at chest height and slightly away from his body — the way you would
hold a sleeping hedgehog: carefully, respectfully, at arm's length. He is
looking at Richard, not at the bundle.

**Behind the counter** — RICHARD, both hands flat on the counter top, leaning
in very slightly, listening. **No apron yet** — the apron is three shots away
and must not appear early. A head and shoulders taller than any child.

**On the counter, customer end** — MACSEN, a ginger cat, sitting upright and
square with his tail curled round his feet, staring directly at Tom with
undisguised judgement.

No children in frame yet. The bundle is still wrapped; the music box is not
visible.

Light: honey arcade light through the front glass, warm lamp on the counter,
the workshop arch glowing warmer still beyond.

## KF-A1.2 — The wrong tune
Reference: KF-A1.1 once approved. Same room, same camera, same light.

The tea towel is now open on the counter and the **walnut music box** stands on
it with its lid raised, showing the brass cylinder and the steel comb. Tom's
right hand is still on the small brass key at the back. The small worn place on
the lid — where a thumb has rested for a hundred years — must read.

The four children have arrived at the counter, drawn by the sound, at three
depths and each doing one thing:

- **PEGGI**, nearest the box, up on her toes with both hands gripping the
  counter edge, chin barely clearing the counter top, delighted. The counter
  must be tall enough against her that her arrival is funny.
- **ALBERT**, beside her, notebook open, pencil already moving, eyes on the
  mechanism.
- **ROSE**, slightly back and to the side, looking at **the lid** and not at
  the mechanism — the only child whose gaze is somewhere different.
- **LILY**, furthest back, arms folded, watching Tom's face rather than the box.

Macsen has not moved. Richard has not moved. Nobody is standing in a row.

[style block]

## KF-A1.3 — The apron
Reference: KF-A1.2 once approved. Same room, same camera, same light.

**RICHARD, mid-ritual, and this is the whole frame:** the apron is already over
his head and tied at the back, and **both hands are smoothing down the front of
it**, flat-palmed, from chest to waist — like a knight settling his armour. His
eyes are already on the music box. He has **not yet touched it**; the box still
sits open on the tea towel.

The four children have gone quiet and are watching him, not the box. Tom watches
too, not understanding what he is seeing.

This is the series ritual's first appearance. The hands on the apron front are
the shot.

## KF-A1.5 — Together
Reference: KF-A1.4 once approved. Same room, same lighting, door still closed
with the seam burning.

**LILY has both hands on the brass ring of the oak door**, feet braced, weight
back, mid-heave — the door is **still shut**; she has not yet pulled it open.
The other three are close behind her: ALBERT with the compass now in his hand,
ROSE beside him with her chin lifted, PEGGI at the front and lowest, both fists
closed with anticipation.

Richard is at the bench in the background, already turning back to his loupe,
not watching them go.

The golden light has not yet poured in. This frame is the half-second before.
