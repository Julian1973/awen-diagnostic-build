# The stress test we never ran

**Raised 2026-08-17**, from the Higgsfield pipeline breakdown. It is the one
stage in that system we have no equivalent for, and it is the one that would
have paid for itself twice already today.

## The rule

> Before any asset is trusted, it generates under combat conditions: different
> angles and shot sizes, the lighting of its actual scenes, and standing next to
> every asset it will share a frame with. Characters have to hit **10 out of 10
> repeatability**. Anything below that keeps the row at draft, and the scene it
> blocks stays closed.
>
> The tests are cheap static images, run before a single expensive video
> generation.

## What it would have caught

**Tom's smile (NEG-002).** He came back smiling through three consecutive shots
about his grandmother's broken heirloom. We found it in dailies, after six
video renders. A test sheet of Tom at five angles under the shop's amber light
would have shown the sheet's default expression is pleasant, and the fix —
directing the face explicitly — would have gone in before a frame moved.

**The bundle shape.** FR01's bundle is a soft round gathered cloth; FR03's is a
crisp rectangular parcel. A prop tested at three angles beside its owner would
have shown the description permitted both, and the canon would have been
corrected before either shot existed.

Both were found on screen, at render cost, by Julian. Neither needed to be.

## What ours would look like

Cheap stills, no video, before Act Two opens:

| Test | What it proves |
|---|---|
| Each character at wide, mid and close | the sheet survives shot size |
| Each character front, three-quarter, profile | it survives angle |
| Each character under the shop's amber lamplight | it survives the scene's own light, not the sheet's neutral ground |
| Each character in a two-shot beside every co-star they share a frame with | identity holds when the model has two faces to keep apart |
| Each hero prop at three angles, in the hands that hold it | the description permits only one object |

Ten runs per asset. Ten passes or the row stays draft.

## Why it matters more for us than for them

Their pipeline generates faces from sheets every time. Ours does too — the
sheets travel as Images 2..N on every single shot. **An untested sheet is a
fault multiplied by every shot the character appears in.** Tom is in eleven of
Scene 1's eighteen shots.

## Status

**Not built.** This is the honest gap. Everything else in that article we either
already do or have deliberately decided against; this one we simply never
thought of, and it belongs in front of Act Two rather than behind it.
