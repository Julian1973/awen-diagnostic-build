## 1 · SCRIPT TRUTH
Thirteen beats, ~110 seconds. Dialogue verbatim, in order, nothing dropped:

1. TOM: *Are you Mr Thistlewood? A lady at the market said — if it's broken, it belongs here.*
2. RICHARD: *Somebody's been reading my sign.*
3. Tom unwraps the box. Sets it down **like an apology**.
4. TOM: *It was my gran's. Hers before that, right back. She left it to me with one instruction.*
5. TOM: *Have it mended. I want it to play again. Only — it's never played right. Listen.*
6. He winds it. The French tune — sweet, and wrong, the same notes bent the same way.
7. The four children materialise at the counter.
8. PEGGI: *Ooh, that's terrible.*
9. ALBERT: *It's not terrible, it's — specific. The same notes are wrong every single time.*
10. ROSE: *Somebody loved this. Loved it loads.*
11. TOM: *Three menders looked at it. Two said scrap it. One said the mechanism's fighting itself — built wrong on purpose. I just want to do what she asked. And I can't even get it to play a proper song.*
12. Richard ties his apron, lifts the box, goes very still. RICHARD: *Nothing is truly broken. It just needs some TLC. Come back at collecting time.*
13. TOM: *Don't you need to know anything about it?* / RICHARD: *I expect I'll find out.* Bell.

**What this unit must make the audience feel:** that a man has walked in carrying
something he loves and cannot explain, and been *believed*.

---

## 2 · DIRECTOR'S INTERPRETATION

**The ache, and its mechanism.** Not a joke — a gap between *duty* and *capacity*.
Tom was given one instruction by someone he loved and he cannot carry it out. The
ache is not that the box is broken; it is that he thinks **he** has failed her.
Every mender told him it was worthless. He came anyway.

**Status.** Tom enters at the bottom — apologetic, standing too far back, braced
for a fourth refusal. Richard never takes status from him. The turn is that
Richard *lowers* himself to the object: he doesn't examine it, he doesn't price
it, he puts on an apron. **Status shifts by service, not authority.** That is the
show.

**Thirds.**
- First third: *embarrassment* — a man apologising for a thing he brought.
- Middle third: *curiosity* — the tune is wrong in a way that is interesting, and
  four children arrive to prove it.
- Last third: *relief* — someone has taken it seriously. Tom does not understand
  why that undoes him slightly. Neither does the audience yet.

**The heart under it.** *"If it's broken, it belongs here."* The sign is not a
slogan; it is a promise, and this unit is the promise being kept for the first
time on screen. Nobody is a villain — not even the three menders. They simply
looked at the mechanism. Rose looks at the lid.

## 3 · PERFORMANCE CHOREOGRAPHY — the clocks

**THE APOLOGY CLOCK** (beats 3–5) · 18s
`SETUP` Tom's hands fold the cloth open — 3s ·
`IMPACT` he sets the box down and **his hands do not immediately let go**, a
half-beat too long — 1s ·
`RECOVERY HOLD` hands withdraw, box alone on the open cloth, static — 1.5s ·
`BUTTON` he looks down at it while he speaks, not at Richard.
*The whole character is in the half-beat where he can't let go.*

**THE WRONG-TUNE CLOCK** (beat 6) · 9s
`SETUP` thumb and finger turn the brass key, twice — 3s ·
`IMPACT` the hand withdraws and the cylinder starts to turn — 1s ·
`RECOVERY HOLD` the mechanism alone, no faces, turning — 3s ·
`BUTTON` it keeps going, patiently, being wrong.
*No face in this shot. The audience listens to an object.*

**THE APRON CLOCK** (beat 12) · 10s — **the series ritual, first appearance**
`SETUP` he reaches off-frame for it — 1s ·
`IMPACT` over the head, tied behind, then **both flat palms smooth the front,
chest to waist** — 4s ·
`RECOVERY HOLD` he lifts the box in both hands and **goes completely still**,
eyes down, 2 full seconds of nothing — 2s ·
`BUTTON` his eyes change. He knows something. He says nothing.
*The hold IS the shot. Anything that shortens it kills the gesture.*

**Silences with named actions** (no dead air):
- After Tom sets the box down → his hands hover, then withdraw.
- After the tune starts → Richard's head tilts a quarter-inch, once.
- After *"built wrong on purpose"* → Peggi looks at Albert, not the box.
- After the apron is smoothed → the two-second stillness. Named, and protected.

**One wordless held beat:** the apron stillness. This unit's nadir and its turn.

## 6 · SPLIT JUDGEMENT — argued

**For one long take:** the scene is continuous in time and space; cuts risk
identity mismatch between shots; fewer generations is cheaper.

**Against, and decisive:** NEG-001 proved 15s with five characters acting in
sequence loses identity outright. But the real argument is not the model's
limit — it is that **two of Tom's speeches are longer than the maximum take**.
His gran speech runs ~15.5s and the menders speech ~16.7s, against a 15s ceiling.
The script itself forbids the single take. Coverage is not a workaround here; it
is the only way the recorded dialogue fits.

**Ruled: eleven shots across six setups.** Every shot but FR-6 has one or two
characters moving, which puts the whole unit inside the motion budget by
construction rather than by luck.
