# COLD OPEN `[0:00 – 1:20]`

## CO.1 — Through the arch `[0:00]` · EXT → INT ARCADE
Fine Welsh rain; the castle, ancient among the buses. Between two shopfronts a
narrow iron arch, easy to miss. **The camera drifts through — and grey rain
turns to gold.** One move, one transition; the whole series premise is in it.

Inside: honey light under the glass roof. Macsen the cat asleep in the
bookshop window, **first shop on the right**. Past him, past the little music
shop, three shops up: gold letters that have never faded —
**A. THISTLEWOOD — RESTORATIONS & CURIOSITIES. EST. 1922.**

**End state:** the shopfront held, lettering legible.
**⚠ BLOCKED — see conflicts below.** Script says right-hand side; the v5 lock
says left. Do not generate an arcade plate until Julian rules.

## CO.2 — Attempt Forty-Seven `[0:35]` · WORKSHOP
Richard **on a stepladder** at the back door, making a tiny adjustment to a
brass panel in the frame. Pleased with himself.
Across the room, in one wide: **Albert** facing the door, stopwatch up,
notebook open, deadly serious. **Peggi** cross-legged **on the workbench**
eating an apple, watching him like a slow bus. **Lily** on the shelves, arms
folded, one scuffed knee. **Rose** in the good chair, drawing Macsen from
memory.

{Attempt forty-seven. Conditions: normal. Barometric pressure —}
{Probably fine.} *(mouth full)*
{The System is *delicate*.} *(wounded)*

He knocks a careful rhythm. Nothing. Consults the notebook. Different rhythm.
Nothing.

{There's nothing on the bench.}
Albert looks: one girl, one apple, no antiques. Closes the stopwatch with
dignity. {…The System also knew that.}

**End state:** the door shut and inert; the bench holding nothing but Peggi.
**Engine:** the rule delivered as a joke. Everything after this pays it off.

## CO.3 — The clothes runner + the bell `[1:05]` · WORKSHOP
Richard, **not turning**: {The door doesn't open for cleverness. Never has.}
Climbing down, pleased: {And I've very nearly got the *clothes* sorted. One
more tweak and it'll dress you properly.}
{You said that last week, Grandad.}
{And I'm *closer* than I was last week.}

He is not. The door knows it. **The shop BELL rings.**

**End state:** four heads turning toward the front room on the bell.

# ACT ONE `[1:20 – 4:00]`

## A1.1 — "If it's broken, it belongs here" `[1:25]` · FRONT ROOM
**Tom Chen** (30s, gentle, lost) holds something wrapped in a tea towel like a
sleeping hedgehog. **Macsen has relocated to the counter to judge him** — the
cat is a performer here, not set dressing.

{Are you Mr Thistlewood? A lady at the market said — if it's broken, it
belongs here.}
{Somebody's been reading my sign.}

He unwraps the **music box**: worn walnut, small brass key, and **a little
worn place on the lid where a thumb has rested for a hundred years.** He sets
it down like an apology.

{It was my gran's. Hers before that, right back. She left it to me with one
instruction. *Have it mended. I want it to play again.* Only — it's never
played *right*. Listen.}

**End state:** the box on the counter, the worn place readable in frame.

## A1.2 — The wrong tune `[2:00]` · FRONT ROOM
He winds it. **A French tune spills out — sweet, and *wrong*. The same notes
bent the same way, every time.** The four children materialise at the counter,
drawn by it.

> HERO-PROP LAW. Not broken. Not clunk-and-jangle. Beautiful and crooked, and
> crooked *identically* on every repeat. If a take reads as "faulty", the take
> is wrong however good it looks — the episode's whole mystery dies with it.

{Ooh, that's *terrible*.} *(delighted)*
{It's not terrible, it's — *specific*. The same notes are wrong every single
time.} *(already writing)*
{Somebody loved this. Loved it *loads*.} *(Rose — **not** looking at the
mechanism, looking at the worn place on the lid)*
{Three menders looked at it. Two said scrap it. One said the mechanism's
*fighting itself* — built wrong on purpose.} *(quiet)* {I just want to do what
she asked. And I can't even get it to play a proper song.}

**Note:** the script runs Rose's line **before** Tom's "three menders" speech;
the manuscript runs it after. Script order stands.
**End state:** the tune dying; Tom's eyes down on the box.

## A1.3 — The apron `[2:50]` · FRONT ROOM
Richard ties his apron — **over the head, tied at back, both hands smoothing
the front** — and lifts the box in his broad careful hands. **He goes very
still. His eyes change, just a little.** He knows something. He doesn't say
what.

{Nothing is truly broken. It just needs some TLC.} *(to the box more than to
Tom)* {Come back at collecting time.}
{Don't you need to know anything about it?}
{I expect I'll find out.}

**End state:** the box in Richard's hands, Tom already half-turned to go.
**Series ritual — its first appearance sets it forever.**

## A1.4 — The door exhales `[3:15]` · WORKSHOP
The bell rings Tom out. **And the BACK DOOR exhales — a thin seam of *other*
light around its edge.**
{It's open.} *(hushed)*

Richard slides the drawer, lifts out the **compass** — brass, needle asleep —
and hands it to Albert **like a coat**. Then reaches up and gives the brass
panel one last hopeful tweak.
{*This* time the clothes will be spot on. I've recalibrated.}
{What's missing this time, Grandad?} *(already rolling up her sleeves)*
{A small thing. You'll know it when you understand it. One hour.} *(back at
his loupe)*

**End state:** compass in Albert's hand, seam of light widening.
**Continuity:** the bench is no longer empty — the precondition CO.2 made a
joke of is now met. That is why the door opened.

## A1.5 — Together `[3:45]` · WORKSHOP
Lily grabs the brass ring two-handed and heaves. **Golden light pours in.**
{Together?} *(breathless)*
{Together.} *(all four)*
They step through. Richard watches the door close. Under his breath, almost
nothing: {Don't be long.} `[4:00]`

**End state:** the door shut, the workshop ordinary again — one old man, one
lamp, one waiting music box. Act Two opens on sun, dust, bread and geese.
