# EPISODE 1 · THE WRONG TUNE · ACT ONE — beat breakdown
Source: Book 1 manuscript, Chapters One and Two, verbatim.
Authority: `canon/script_authority.json`. Geometry and scale from `canon/continuity_locks.json`.
Target: ~5 minutes (series architecture: Act 1 = object arrives, door opens).

**This supersedes MP.B1.** MP.B1 was directed without the manuscript and
invented its opening; it is kept only as the record of that error.

Rooms are two, and the distinction is load-bearing:
- **WORKSHOP** — shelves/radio/stove/kettle LEFT, workbench + brick window
  RIGHT, oak door with the iron T centred in the stone rear wall, the good
  chair by the window. **No counter.**
- **SHOP FLOOR** — through the arch. **Counter rear right, facing the
  entrance.** The bell is above the front door. Tom Chen never enters the
  workshop.

## E1.A1.B1 — "Attempt Forty Seven" · WORKSHOP
The four at the back of the workshop, staring at the door. Three staring at
the door; Albert staring at his notebook.

Albert, stopwatch in one hand, pencil behind his ear, with the seriousness of
a man defusing a bomb: {Attempt forty seven. Conditions: normal. Barometric
pressure…}
Peggi — cross-legged **on the workbench**, mouth full of apple, watching him
the way you watch a very slow bus that has taken a wrong turning:
{Probably fine.}
Albert, wounded: {The System is delicate.}

He knocks: a careful little rhythm, rat-a-tat-TAT. The door does nothing. It
is very good at doing nothing. He consults the notebook, knocks a completely
different rhythm. Nothing.

Peggi: {Want me to tell you why it's not working?}
Albert: {The System will tell me why it's not working.}
Peggi: {There's nothing on the bench.}

Albert looks at the bench: one girl, one apple, four apple pips, no broken
antiques whatsoever. He closes the stopwatch with tremendous dignity.
{…The System also knew that.}

**End state:** the door shut and inert; the bench holding nothing but Peggi.
**Engine:** the rule stated as a joke — the door opens for a broken thing, not
for cleverness. Everything Act 1 does afterwards is that rule paying off.

## E1.A1.B2 — "Never has" · WORKSHOP
Richard at the top of a **stepladder** beside the doorframe, making a very
small adjustment to a little brass panel with a very small screwdriver. He
speaks without turning round.
{The door doesn't open for cleverness, Albert. Never has.}
One last tiny adjustment. He climbs down, pleased with himself.
{And I'll tell you something else. I've very nearly got the clothes sorted.
One more tweak and it'll dress you properly.}

Lily — eldest, against the shelves, arms folded, a scuff on each knee —
exchanges a look with Rose. Rose is in **the good chair by the window**,
carefully drawing Macsen from memory; she adds one eye half open, because
Macsen never sleeps so deeply that he misses anything.

Lily: {You said that last week, Grandad.}
Richard: {And I'm closer than I was last week.}

He is not. The door knows it. The children suspect it. Nobody says so —
because the bell at the front of the shop gives one bright, important *ting*.

**End state:** four heads turning toward the shop floor on the bell.
**Note:** Rose's drawing and Lily's scuffed knees are character, not set
dressing. Do not substitute generic business.

## E1.A1.B3 — "If it's broken, it belongs here" · SHOP FLOOR, COUNTER
A man at the counter holding something wrapped in a tea towel — the way you
hold a sleeping hedgehog: carefully, respectfully, slightly at arm's length.
{Are you Mr Thistlewood? A lady at the market said that if it's broken, it
belongs here.}
Richard: {Somebody's been reading my sign.}

He is **Tom Chen**. He sets the bundle on the counter like an apology.
{It was my gran's. And her gran's before that. It goes right back. She left it
to me with one instruction.} — a swallow — {Have it mended. I want it to play
again. But that's the thing. It's never played right. Listen.}

**End state:** the tea towel open on the counter, the walnut box revealed —
worn smooth and dark at the corners by a hundred years of hands, small brass
key at the back.

## E1.A1.B4 — The wrong tune · SHOP FLOOR
He winds the key. The lid lifts. Out comes **a French tune, sweet and
unmistakably wrong**.

> Not broken wrong. Not clunk and jangle wrong. The notes bend in odd places,
> the way a picture hangs crooked. Worse still, they bend in **exactly the
> same places every time**.

This is the single most important sound in the episode and the easiest to get
wrong. It must read as *beautiful and crooked*, never as *faulty*.

Peggi appears at the counter as if by magic, delighted: {Ooh. That's terrible.}
Albert, already writing: {It's not terrible. It's oddly specific. The same
notes are wrong every time. Listen. There… and there…}
Tom: {Three menders have looked at it. Two told me to scrap it. One said the
mechanism was fighting itself, as if somebody had built it wrongly on purpose.
But that makes no sense.} — quieter, looking down at the box — {I just want to
do what she asked. And I can't even get it to play a proper song.}

**End state:** the tune dying away; Tom's eyes down on the box.

## E1.A1.B5 — "Somebody loved this" · SHOP FLOOR
Rose comes to stand beside Peggi. She does **not** look at the mechanism. She
looks at the lid — at the tiny worn place where thumb after thumb has rubbed
the varnish away, always in the same spot, over years and years and years.
{Somebody loved this. Loved it loads.}

**End state:** the frame holding on the worn thumb-place. This is the shot the
whole episode rhymes with; it returns in Ch.7 under Richard's cloth and in
Ch.8 under Tom's thumb.

## E1.A1.B6 — The apron · SHOP FLOOR → WORKSHOP
Richard reaches for his apron. The children have seen it a thousand times and
it is always the same: **over the head, tied at the back, then both hands
smoothing the front, like a knight settling his armour.**

Then, and only then, does he lift the music box in his broad, careful hands.

And he goes very still. His eyes change, only a little — the way the sky
changes when a cloud you cannot see passes across the sun. He knows something.
He does not say what. He never says what.

Richard, to the box more than to Tom: {Nothing is truly broken. It just needs
some TLC.} He sets it gently on the workbench. {Come back at collecting time.}
Tom: {Don't you need to know anything about it? Where it's from, or…}
Richard: {I expect I'll find out.}

**End state:** the box on the workbench under the lamp. The bench is no longer
empty — which is the precondition B1 established as a joke.

## E1.A1.B7 — The door breathes out · WORKSHOP
The bell rings Tom out into the golden arcade. The moment the shop door clicks
shut, the old oak door at the back of the workshop **breathes out**: a thin
seam of light, the colour of somewhere else entirely, kindles around its edge.

The four go absolutely still. Albert, whispering: {It's open.}

Richard slides open the **workbench drawer** and lifts out the compass — old
brass, small enough to sit in your palm, its needle fast asleep. He passes it
to Albert without a word, the way you might hand someone their coat. Then he
reaches up and gives the little brass panel one final, hopeful tweak.
{There. This time the clothes will be spot on. I've recalibrated.}
Lily, already rolling up her sleeves: {What's missing this time, Grandad?}
Richard: {A small thing. You'll know it when you understand it.}
He picks up his loupe and turns back to the bench, as if four children stepping
through a magic door into the past were the least remarkable part of his
Saturday. {One hour.}

Lily seizes the brass ring with both hands and heaves. Golden light pours into
the workshop, bright and bold as trumpet music.
Rose, suddenly breathless: {Together?}
The other three: {Together.}
They step through.

Behind them, alone, Richard watches the door swing shut and speaks so softly
that only the door could have heard him: {Don't be long.}

**End state:** the door shut, the workshop ordinary again, one old man and one
mended-in-waiting music box under a lamp. Act 2 opens on the smell of bread,
horses and summer dust.
