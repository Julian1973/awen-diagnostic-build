Production OS · provider-agnostic

Studio Memory

One script in, one film out, on any stack. Three systems merged, and every disagreement written down rather than smoothed over.

The premise

The model has no memory between generations, so the pipeline is the memory. A face is redrawn from scratch every time; if what he looks like is not in the request, the request gets a different man.

And the sentence that prevents the most damage: a prompt is a list of things the model may draw, so anything it names that is not in the frame is a gap the model will fill.

The four rules

  1. Nothing generates for the film until every asset it uses is locked.
  2. One asset, one passport — descriptor plus files, copied verbatim, never shortened.
  3. Edits are surgical: one declared change per version, everything else byte-identical.
  4. Everything versioned and logged, because an unlogged good shot cannot be reproduced.

The laws, and what each one cost

No dialogue words in a prompt

The route generates speech from the prompt and animates the mouth to what it invented. Our recording then plays over the top, and the two can never agree. The mouth is directed as a talking rhythm, and the prompt says why — so nobody helpfully puts the line back.

the voice deteriorated, then a section arrived completely different. A mish-mash.

The speaker box

A lipsync service takes a video and an audio file, finds a face and drives it. Not one route measured accepts a face selector. So a multi-face shot declares the region holding the speaker and nobody else: crop, upscale, sync, composite back at the same coordinates.

one man's line was animated onto the other man's mouth — the speaker law failing one stage further downstream than the speaker law could reach.

Describe the frame, not the location

Every shot declares how much of its location it actually shows: full, partial, or none. The test before writing any scene clause is whether the thing is visible in the frame. On a tight frame, say so explicitly — silence is a gap too.

a counter-top insert was handed the standard clause reciting the whole shop, and built a bright kitchen with a window in it. The keyframe was not ignored — it was completed.

The audit is a loop, not a stamp

Score, correct, then re-score the corrected text. Rounds are stamped against a hash of the exact words they scored, and only a round matching the current text counts.

a prompt was rewritten materially and fired against the audit of the version before the change.

Direct the face

Body comes from the secondary-life block, voice from the delivery note, the mouth from the speaker law — and an expression left unstated gets filled in with the average of every similar scene, which is pleasant. Where the beat needs it, say not smiling.

a man smiled warmly through three consecutive shots about his grandmother's broken heirloom.

Scale by body landmark, never by number

"Her head reaches his shoulder" survives; a measurement does not. Metres for location geometry are fine — the ban is on bodies.

heights in centimetres came back with the ladder inverted and the largest gap rendered as no gap at all.

Chain the end frame

The previous take's last frame is the next shot's Image 1 — continuity when the camera cuts, composition when it holds. The room cannot drift when Image 1 is the previous frame rather than a fresh generation of the same room. Generate keyframes per setup, not per shot, and have a human make them.

a generated keyframe turned an archway into a curtained recess and lost a door bell. The hand-made one held every lock.

Approval does not survive a set change

When a plate, a lock or a script line moves, every approved asset downstream is provisional again. Review a scene on one contact sheet, not shot by shot.

the one approved take was the only shot nobody rebuilt when the room changed — because it was the one shot everybody trusted. Sixteen individual reviews missed it; one contact sheet caught it in four seconds.

Where this overrules its sources

Both source systems assume the video model lip-syncs

The model generates the voice, and a sound team cleans that same voice afterwards. For a production with a cast — recorded voice IDs, directed performances — that is backwards. The recording should drive the mouth, not the other way round.

Hence stage 07, which exists in neither of them.

The open question, deliberately unresolved

No negative prompts

Higgsfield writes every prohibition as what is in frame, with no "do not" anywhere. Ours are full of them, and are currently producing approved takes.

That is a real methodological difference and it deserves a controlled A/B on one shot — not a compiler rewrite on the strength of an article.
