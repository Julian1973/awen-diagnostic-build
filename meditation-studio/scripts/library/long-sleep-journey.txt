## Welcome
[softly] Hello, and welcome. This is a long, slow journey down into sleep. You can stay with my voice for as long as you like, and let it fade whenever you are ready. There is nothing you need to do, and nothing you need to remember.

Settle into the bed in whatever way feels best for your body tonight. On your back, on your side, curled up. Let the pillow take the weight of your head. Let the covers be just right.

Take a slow breath in through the nose... and let it out through the mouth, like a long, quiet sigh.

Again, breathing in... and letting it all go.

[[silence 6s]]

Let the next breath out be a little longer than the breath in. And the next one. A long, slow out breath tells the body that it is safe to rest. It tells the busy part of the mind that the day is done.

## Counting down
[softly] [slowly] In a moment, I'll count slowly down from ten to one. With each number, let yourself sink a little deeper into the bed. You don't need to try. Just let each number carry you down, the way a leaf drifts down through still air.

Ten. The shoulders softening.

Nine. The arms growing heavy.

Eight. The hands still and loose.

Seven. The chest rising and falling, all by itself.

Six. The belly soft.

[[silence 8s]]

Five. The hips and the legs sinking down.

Four. The feet warm and heavy.

Three. The face smooth. The jaw loose.

Two. The whole body heavy, held, and safe.

One. All the way down.

[[silence 20s]]

## Letting the day go
[softly] [slowly] If your mind is still busy, that's alright. Minds are busy. You don't have to make it stop. You only need to stop following it for a while.

Picture the thoughts of the day as boats on a wide harbour, tied up for the night. Each one has its place. The conversations, the worries, the list of things still to do. They are all moored safely. They will still be there in the morning, if you need them. For now, you can let them bob gently on the water while you walk away.

[[silence 20s]]

Whatever didn't get done today can wait. Whatever was said, or wasn't said, can wait. You have done enough for one day. You are allowed to put it all down.

[[silence 30s]]

Feel the weight of the body pressing into the bed. Heavier with every breath out. As if the bed is gently holding you, and you can finally stop holding yourself.

[[silence 30s]]

## Softening the body
[softly] [slowly] Now let's soften the body, one part at a time. Nothing to do. Only noticing, and letting go.

The scalp, and the tiny muscles around the ears. Let them soften. The forehead, smooth and wide. The eyes, resting back in their sockets. The cheeks. The jaw, the teeth slightly apart, the tongue lying soft in the mouth.

[[silence 15s]]

The throat, open and easy. The shoulders, dropping down and away. The upper arms, the elbows, the forearms. The hands, heavy and warm, the fingers curled loosely.

[[silence 15s]]

The chest, rising and falling. The belly, soft. The whole of the back, spreading out against the bed. The hips. The thighs, the knees, the calves. The ankles, the heels, the soles of the feet. All of it soft. All of it resting.

[[silence 40s]]

## The lane
[softly] [slowly] Now imagine you are standing at the door of an old stone cottage on a warm summer evening. The light is fading. The air smells of cut grass and the sea.

In front of you, a narrow lane winds down the hill between high hedges full of honeysuckle and foxgloves. You begin to walk, slowly, with no need to hurry. Your feet know the way.

[[silence 20s]]

With each step, the lane goes a little further down. The hedges are tall and soft on either side. Somewhere a blackbird is singing its last song of the day. A gate creaks in the breeze and is still again.

[[silence 30s]]

You come to a little stone bridge over a stream. You stop for a moment and lean on the wall. The stones are still warm from the sun. Below, the water runs clear and slow over the pebbles, talking quietly to itself. You listen for a while.

[[silence 40s]]

On the other side of the bridge there is a cottage with a lamp lit in the window, and a thin line of woodsmoke rising straight up into the still air. Someone inside is settling down for the night, just as you are. Everything around you is slowing down.

[[silence 40s]]

The lane bends, and between the hedges you catch a glimpse of the sea below. It is calm tonight, silver and grey, barely moving. The sky above it is turning a deep, soft blue.

[[silence 30s]]

You walk on. Down past a small white chapel. Down past a field where cows are lying in the long grass. Down, and down, and the air grows cooler and quieter.

[[silence 40s]]

## The bay
[softly] [slowly] At the bottom of the lane there is a small, sheltered bay. The tide is out, and the sand is smooth and pale. Your feet sink into it a little with every step. It is soft and cool.

The only sound is the sea, folding over and over at the edge of the sand. In... and out. In... and out. Like breathing.

[[silence 40s]]

Near the water there is a little wooden boat, pulled up on the sand, with a blanket folded inside it. You climb in and lie down. The blanket is warm. The wood is smooth beneath you. Above you, the first stars are appearing, one by one.

[[silence 40s]]

You might like to count them, slowly. One star. Two. Three. A few more, there over the headland. Don't worry if you lose count. There are always more stars than you can count, and you don't need to count them all.

[[silence 45s]]

Far across the water, on the point, a lighthouse turns. Its beam sweeps slowly over the sea, round and round, and every time it passes it seems to say, all is well, all is well. You watch it turn, and your eyes grow heavier each time.

[[silence 60s]]

As you lie there, the tide begins to come in. So slowly that you hardly notice. The water lifts the boat, gently, and holds it. You are floating now, rocking softly on the calm water, close to the shore, quite safe.

[[silence 60s]]

The boat rises and falls with the sea. Rises... and falls. Your breath rises and falls with it. There is nothing to steer. Nowhere to go. The sea knows how to hold you.

[[silence 60s]]

## Deeper
[softly] [slowly] Feel how heavy the body has become. How warm. How still. Each breath out lets you sink a little further into the blanket, into the boat, into the slow rocking of the water.

[[silence 60s]]

Let's go down once more, from five to one, even more slowly this time.

Five... the sea rocking you.

Four... the stars bright above.

Three... the blanket warm around you.

Two... the shore close and safe.

One... all the way down, into rest.

[[silence 90s]]

If you find yourself awake, there is nothing wrong. Just rest. Resting is good for you too. Let the sound of the sea carry you.

[[silence 90s]]

The stars are bright now. The sea is quiet. The shore is close. You are safe, and warm, and held.

[[silence 120s]]

Rising... and falling...

[[silence 150s]]

Deeper... and softer...

[[silence 180s]]

Sleep now.
